Editing clips

Once actions are on the timeline, timing is a drag away. Slide clips, trim their edges, stretch steps, and tune parameters inline, all with full undo.

Moving and resizing clips

A clip on the timeline is the span between two actions: its opening action (a Reveal, Play Video, or Play Audio) and its closing action (a Hide or Stop). Dragging edits those actions directly:

  • Drag the clip body to slide the whole clip in time. Both the opening and closing actions move together by the same amount, so the clip keeps its length.
  • Drag an edge to retime just one endpoint. Each clip has a thin grab zone at its left and right edge; the left edge moves the opening action, the right edge moves the closing one. Chapter Studio clamps the drag so the opening can never pass the closing.

While you drag, a live tooltip rides above the timeline showing the exact time (→ 5.20s for a body drag, or start 4.10s / end 7.30s when resizing an edge), so you can land on a precise beat without guessing from pixels.

Note. On video clips, edge drags do a little more: the right edge also sets the clip's source out-point. See video trimming & cropping.

Crossing step boundaries

Clips aren't fenced inside their step. Drag a clip (or an edge) across a step boundary and Chapter Studio moves the underlying action into the destination step for you, converting between a step-start action and a scheduled action as needed. You think in seconds; Chapter Studio keeps the step structure bookkeeping straight underneath.

Each completed drag records exactly one undo entry. A long, fiddly drag is still a single ⌘Z away from being undone, never a stack of micro-steps.

The clip context menu

Right-click any clip for quick operations:

CommandWhat it does
Reveal in InspectorSelects the clip's source step and asset so the inspector jumps straight to its actions.
Duplicate at +0.5sCopies the opening action half a second later, handy for echoing a beat or building a repeated pattern.
Delete Opening ActionRemoves the action that opens the clip. The clip disappears from the timeline (its closing action, if any, no longer has anything to close).

Retiming steps on the ruler

The steps themselves can stretch, too. Every interior step boundary on the time ruler is a draggable handle: pull it left or right to shorten or lengthen the step that ends there. Steps are clamped to a minimum of 0.1 seconds, so you can't accidentally collapse a beat out of existence.

The clip inspector

Select an asset within a segment (clicking one of its clips is the quickest way) and the right-hand inspector leads with a card listing every action in the segment that targets that asset, in time order. Each row shows the action's glyph and label, a one-line parameter summary (like "opacity 0.5 · 0.50s"), and its absolute time, plus a jump button that selects the source step and a delete button.

Rows for the most-tuned actions carry an expand chevron that opens compact inline controls, so the common adjustments never need a dialog:

ActionInline controls
RevealFade-in duration slider
FadeOpacity + duration sliders
ScaleMultiplier + duration sliders
MoveDuration slider
Play Audio / Play VideoVolume slider + loop toggle
Fade AudioTarget volume + duration sliders

Everything else still shows its summary row, and any action can be opened in its full parameter form, including the raw-JSON view, from the inspector. See the actions reference for what each action exposes.

Tip. The clip inspector is the fastest way to audit an asset's whole arc through a segment: every reveal, move, fade, and hide in one scrollable list, each one click from its source step.

Undo is everywhere

Every edit described on this page — clip drags, edge trims, boundary drags, inline slider changes, context-menu commands — flows through the same undo stack. ⌘Z undoes, ⇧⌘Z redoes, and the history runs deep, so experiment freely: the timeline is a safe place to try a timing idea and walk it back.
