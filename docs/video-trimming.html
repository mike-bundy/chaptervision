Video trimming & cropping

Play just the part of a video you want — trims are non-destructive source windows by default, with a one-way crop tool for when you're ready to shrink the master itself.

Source windows: trims without touching the file

Every video clip can carry a source window — an in-point and an out-point into the master file. The clip plays only that slice: playback cues up at the in-point and stops at the out-point. The master video on disk is never modified and never re-encoded; the window is just timing data stored with the action, so you can widen, narrow, or clear it at any time with zero quality cost.

Both the Studio preview and the player on Vision Pro honor the window — what you scrub on the Mac is exactly what plays on the headset, down to the frame where the clip cues in and where it ends.

Trimming on the timeline

Trims live on the video clip's edges in Studio:

  • Right edge — drag the clip's right edge (plain or with ⌥ held) to set the out-point: the moment in the source where playback stops.
  • ⌥ + left edge — hold Option and drag the left edge to slip the in-point: the clip stays where it is on the timeline, but starts from a later moment in the source. It's the classic film-editing slip — reframe the content without moving the beat.

A plain left-edge drag still retimes when the clip starts in the segment, as with any clip — see editing clips. Think of it as: plain drags edit when, Option drags edit what.

Tip. On Chapter Vision, select a video clip and switch its mode chip to Trim — the left handle becomes the slip and the right handle the out-point, mirroring the Mac's Option-drag behavior.

Crop to Clip Length: shrinking the master

Source windows are perfect while you're iterating, but a two-minute master trimmed down to a ten-second window still ships two minutes of video in your chapter bundle. When the edit has settled, Crop to Clip Length… makes the trim physical:

  1. The windows are rebased. Every clip in the chapter that plays the same master — across all segments — has its source window recalculated against the new, shorter file, so every clip keeps playing exactly the frames it played before.

  2. The file is trimmed with a passthrough export. The video is cut, not re-encoded — no generation loss, no quality change, and it's fast.

  3. The master is replaced atomically. The new file swaps in as a single operation, so there's never a moment where the chapter points at a half-written video.

  4. Content fingerprints refresh. The chapter's asset records are updated so a connected Vision Pro re-fetches the new file immediately — the headset never plays stale frames from a cached copy of the old master.

You'll find the command in two places in Studio — the video clip's right-click menu and the clip inspector — and on Chapter Vision in the selected clip's trim card or by long-pressing the clip.

Watch out. Crop to Clip Length is destructive: the media outside the window is permanently discarded from the master file, and every clip sharing that file is affected. If you might want the discarded footage later, keep a copy of the original video outside the project before cropping.

Choosing between the two

Source window (edge trim)Crop to Clip Length
ReversibleYes — adjust or clear any time No — discarded media is gone
Master fileUntouchedPhysically shortened
QualityUnchangedUnchanged (passthrough, no re-encode)
Bundle sizeFull master shipsOnly the kept slice ships
Best forIterating on an edit Final cleanup before sharing or shipping a chapter

A good rhythm: trim non-destructively while the story is still moving, then crop the masters as a last pass — your .chapterscript bundle gets lighter, loads faster over live sync, and plays identically.
